# Culinary Provenance: Evidence And Interpretation

## Scope

Five ingredients and twelve episodes form a curated prototype, not a complete crop history. Evidence was reviewed September 25, 2026. Data are bundled in `../data/ingredients.js`. Each record includes its botanical scope, an origin interpretation, coordinates, a source, and chronological episodes with dates, evidence types, explanatory text and source URLs.

Native range, centre of diversity, inferred domestication, and archaeological attestation are distinguished. An origin point anchors a description; it does not imply a geographically exact birthplace. Dated occurrences do not establish first-ever use. The base map shows modern geography throughout.

## Source Families

- Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew: [A Spice Sensation](https://www.kew.org/read-and-watch/marianne-north-gallery-a-spice-sensation) provides nutmeg’s Banda context, the 1621 conquest, Poivre’s Mauritius garden in the 1770s, Bencoolen cultivation by 1820, and Marianne North’s 1883 Seychelles observations. We omit the article’s disputed Grenada introduction date rather than present it as settled.
- Kew: [Christmas Spices](https://www.kew.org/read-and-watch/christmas-spices) supports the clove native-range and botanical-organ descriptions.
- Kew: [Piper nigrum](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn%3Alsid%3Aipni.org%3Anames%3A682369-1/general-information) supports black pepper’s botanical and Western Ghats origin context.
- Roderick Geerts (2023), [Leiden Archaeology](https://www.leidenarchaeologyblog.nl/articles/from-ceramics-to-connectivity-berenike-and-the-indian-ocean-trade), describes Berenike’s Indian Ocean connections and a vessel with 7.55 kg of pepper. The article gives the broad Roman-period context, not an exact shipment year.
- Sonia Zarrillo et al. (2018), [Nature Ecology & Evolution](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-018-0697-x), report approximately 5,300-year-old cacao evidence from Santa Ana–La Florida in southeast Ecuador.
- Claire Lanaud et al. (2024), [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-53010-6), report cacao use outside the Amazonian native area about 5,000 years ago and discuss exchanges with the Pacific coast.
- Kraig H. Kraft et al. (2014), [PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4035960/), combine evidence supporting a central–eastern Mexican origin for domesticated C. annuum. This does not establish one origin for the genus Capsicum.
- Brendan Borrell (2009), [Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/whats-so-hot-about-chili-peppers-116907465/), describes the Atlantic and Portuguese-network dispersal of chillies. Species and individual shipping tracks are not resolved.

## Date And Coordinate Conventions

The slider has seven discrete cutoffs: 4000 BCE, 3000 BCE, 500 CE, 1500, 1650, 1800, and 1900. Positions are equally spaced in the interface but not in elapsed time. Episodes appear when their display-anchor year is at or before the cutoff.

- Approximate cacao ages are rounded to c. 3300 BCE and c. 3000 BCE. The broad Pacific-coast location is not a single excavated site.
- The chilli domestication record uses c. 4000 BCE as a coarse mid-Holocene teaching anchor, not a directly dated domestication event.
- Berenike uses 100 CE within the cited first-to-sixth-century context. Do not interpret this as a known arrival date.
- The 1770s are anchored to 1770. The 1490s Atlantic transfer is anchored to 1493. These support broad period comparisons only.
- The 1883 Seychelles points are observations by an artist, not first-introduction dates.

Coordinates are editorial geographic anchors. Great-circle connections are illustrative and may cross land. They do not reconstruct navigation. Cacao’s archaeological observations are not linked because the evidence does not establish a direct journey between them. Later Seychelles connections show geographic displacement, not a proven direct origin-to-island planting lineage. Chilli’s Atlantic connection begins in Hispaniola; its broad Portuguese-network connection begins in Pernambuco, so neither falsely begins in the Mexican domestication region.
