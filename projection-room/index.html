The Projection Room

One planet. Different geometries. Different compromises.

The gold shapes start as circles of the same 5° angular radius on the globe. Compare their areas and shapes within each panel. These finite geodesic circles illustrate distortion; they are not infinitesimal Tissot indicatrices.

What changes when the world becomes a plane?

◉

Area

An equal-area projection keeps relative areas in proportion. Continents may look unfamiliar because their shapes change.

△

Shape

A conformal projection preserves angles locally. It can greatly enlarge regions far from its standard lines or central point.

↗

Distance

No flat map preserves every distance. A straight line on the map need not be the shortest route on Earth.

Reading the Comparison

Both panels use the same Natural Earth geography and central meridian. Each projection is independently fitted to its frame: do not compare absolute pixel areas across panels. Mercator is clipped near ±85.05° latitude; Orthographic shows only the near hemisphere. The other six views show the whole world.

These are spherical models of Earth. The controls change real projection mathematics, not a deformation of a flat image. The circles are sampled with a 5° spherical radius at 30° intervals of longitude and latitude, excluding the poles.
