# Names of India

Names of India

"Bharat (term)" redirects here. For other uses, see [Bharat (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharat_(disambiguation) "Bharat (disambiguation)").

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/India_78.40398E_20.74980N.jpg/500px-India_78.40398E_20.74980N.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:India_78.40398E_20.74980N.jpg) Relief map of the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent"), centred on the [Republic of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India").

The [**Republic of India**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") is principally known by two official short names: **India** and **Bharat**. As per Article 300 of the [Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India "Constitution of India"), the [Government of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India "Government of India") may be referred to as the **Union of India** in legal proceedings. An unofficial third name is **[Hindustan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan "Hindustan")**, which is widely used throughout [North India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_India "North India"). Although these names now refer to the modern country in most contexts, they historically denoted the broader [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent").

## Overview

"India" ( [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language "Greek language"): Ἰνδία) is a name derived from the [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River "Indus River") and remains the country's common name in the [Western world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_world "Western world"), having been used by the [ancient Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece%E2%80%93Ancient_India_relations "Ancient Greece–Ancient India relations") to refer to the lands east of [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia "Persia") and south of the [Himalayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayas "Himalayas"). This name appeared in [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English "Old English") by the 9th century and re-emerged in [Modern English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_English "Modern English") in the 17th century.

The earliest known epigraphical attestations of the name _Bharata_ occurs in the [Hāthigumphā inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hathigumpha_inscription "Hathigumpha inscription") of King [Kharavela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharavela "Kharavela") (2nd-1st century BCE), a [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain "Jain") inscription at [Udayagiri Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udayagiri_Caves "Udayagiri Caves"), near [Bhubaneswar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhubaneswar "Bhubaneswar") in [Odisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odisha "Odisha"). The inscription, which records Kharavela's military campaigns, contains a reference to _Bharatavarsha_, indicating that the term was already in use as a cultural or territorial designation in [ancient India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outline_of_ancient_India "Outline of ancient India").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-1)

According to Jain tradition, the name "Bharatavarsha" derives from [Emperor Bharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharata_chakravarti "Bharata chakravarti"), the eldest son of the first [Tirthankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirthankara "Tirthankara"), [Rishabhdeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishabhanatha "Rishabhanatha") (also named Adinatha or Ikshvāku). Jain texts describe Bharata as a universal monarch ( [chakravartin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakravarti_(Sanskrit_term) "Chakravarti (Sanskrit term)")), after whom the land was named. This association between Bharata and the territorial name "Bharatavarsha" is a recurring theme in [Jain literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain_literature "Jain literature").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-2)[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-3)

According to the [Vedic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas "Vedas") tradition, "Bharat" ( [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi_language "Hindi language"): भारत) is the shortened form of the name "Bharatavarsha" in the [Sanskrit language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit"). It originates from the [Vedic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_period "Vedic period") and is rooted in the [Dharmic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmic_religions "Dharmic religions"), particularly [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"). The long-form Sanskrit name is derived from the [Bharata tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatas_(Vedic_tribe) "Bharatas (Vedic tribe)"), who are mentioned in the [Rigveda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigveda "Rigveda") as one of the principal peoples of [Aryavarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80ry%C4%81varta "Āryāvarta"), which roughly corresponds with the [Indo-Gangetic Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Gangetic_Plain "Indo-Gangetic Plain"). The initial application of the name referred only to the western part of the [Gangetic Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges_Basin "Ganges Basin").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-Jha-4)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-Upinder_Singh-5) In 1949, the [Constituent Assembly of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituent_Assembly_of_India "Constituent Assembly of India") adopted "Bharat" (alongside "India") as one of the country's two official short names, by even explicitly rejecting "Hindustan" (at least as an official name).

"Hindustan" ( [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language"): هندوستان) is also a name derived from the Indus River, combining " [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus#Etymology "Hindus")" as an exonym with the suffix " [-stan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/-stan "-stan")" in the Persian language. It has been the most common Persian name for India since at least the 3rd century, with the earlier form " [Hindush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindush "Hindush")" (an adaptation of the Sanskrit name " [Sindhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River "Indus River")") being attested in [Old Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian "Old Persian") as early as the 6th century BCE, when it was used to refer to the lands east of the [Persian frontier in the Indus Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_conquest_of_the_Indus_Valley "Achaemenid conquest of the Indus Valley"). However, the name did not become particularly widespread in other languages until the 11th century, when it was popularised during the [Muslim period in the Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_period_in_the_Indian_subcontinent "Muslim period in the Indian subcontinent"). While it is no longer used in an official capacity, " [Hindustan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan "Hindustan")" is still a common name for India in the [Hindustani language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustani_language "Hindustani language").

## India

Further information: [India (Herodotus)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_(Herodotus) "India (Herodotus)") and [Indica (Megasthenes)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indica_(Megasthenes) "Indica (Megasthenes)")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Herodotus_world_map-en.svg/500px-Herodotus_world_map-en.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Herodotus_world_map-en.svg) India was the lower [Indus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River "Indus River") basin in [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus "Herodotus")'s view of the world.

The English term is from [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek "Ancient Greek") _Indikē_ (cf. [Megasthenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megasthenes "Megasthenes")' work [Indica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indica_(Megasthenes) "Indica (Megasthenes)")) or _Indía_ ([Ἰνδία](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E1%BC%B8%CE%BD%CE%B4%CE%AF%CE%B1#Ancient_Greek "wikt:Ἰνδία")), via [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin") transliteration _India_.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-8)

The name derives from [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit") _Sindhu_, which was the name of the [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River "Indus River") as well as the lower [Indus Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Basin "Indus Basin").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-Eggermont_Sindhu-10)

The [Old Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian "Old Persian") equivalent of _Síndhu_ was _Hindu_.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-11) [Darius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_I "Darius I") conquered Sindh in about 516 BCE, upon which the Persian equivalent _Hinduš_ was used for the province at the lower Indus basin.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-Eggermont_Hindush-12)[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-Dandamaev_Hindush-13) [Scylax of Caryanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scylax_of_Caryanda "Scylax of Caryanda") who explored the Indus river for the Persian emperor probably took over the Persian name and passed it into Greek.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-14) The terms _Indos_ for the Indus river as well as "an Indian" are found in [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus "Herodotus")' [Histories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histories_(Herodotus) "Histories (Herodotus)").[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-15) The loss of the [aspirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspirated_consonant "Aspirated consonant") /h/ was probably due to the dialects of Greek spoken in [Asia Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Minor "Asia Minor").[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-16)[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-17) Herodotus also generalised the term "Indian" from the people of lower Indus basin, to all the people living to the east of Persia, even though he had no knowledge of the geography of the land.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-18)

By the time of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great"), _Indía_ in [Koine Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koine_Greek "Koine Greek") denoted the region beyond the Indus. Alexander's companions were aware of at least India up to the [Ganges delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges_delta "Ganges delta") ( [Gangaridai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangaridai "Gangaridai")).[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-19)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-Mukherjee-20) Later, [Megasthenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megasthenes "Megasthenes") included in India the southern peninsula as well.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-Mukherjee-20)

Megasthenes was a Greek ambassador in the [Mauryan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauryan_Empire "Mauryan Empire") court during the reign of [Chandragupta Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Maurya "Chandragupta Maurya") and wrote a detailed account of his visit in _[Indica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indica_(Megasthenes) "Indica (Megasthenes)")_. He resided in the Mauryan capital of [Pataliputra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pataliputra "Pataliputra"), noting that they surpassed in power and glory in all India.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKosmin201438-21)[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-AGT-22)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-AH-23)

> They surpass in power and glory every other people, not only in this quarter, but one may say in all India, their capital being [Palibothra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pataliputra "Pataliputra"), a very large and wealthy city, after which some call the people itself the Palibothri, - nay, even the whole tract along the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges "Ganges"). Their king has in his pay a standing army of 600,000 foot-soldiers, 30,000 cavalry, and 9000 elephants: whence may be formed some conjecture as to the vastness of his resources. [Megasthenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megasthenes "Megasthenes"), in [Indica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indica_(Megasthenes) "Indica (Megasthenes)")[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-24)

Latin _India_ is used by [Lucian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucian "Lucian") (2nd century CE).\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]_India_ was known in [Old English language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English_language "Old English language") and was used in [King Alfred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_the_Great "Alfred the Great")'s translation of [Paulus Orosius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulus_Orosius "Paulus Orosius"). In [Middle English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_English "Middle English"), the name was, under French influence, replaced by _Ynde_ or _Inde_, which entered [Early Modern English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Modern_English "Early Modern English") as "Indie". The name "India" then came back to English usage from the 17th century onward, and may be due to the influence of Latin, or Spanish and Portuguese.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

[Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit") [इन्दु॑](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%A4%87%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%81#Sanskrit "wikt:इन्दु") "drop (of [Soma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soma_(drink) "Soma (drink)"))", also a term for the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon "Moon"), is unrelated, but has sometimes been erroneously connected (see [Middle Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Chinese "Middle Chinese") [印度](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%8D%B0%E5%BA%A6#Chinese "wikt:印度") for more).\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

## Indies

The term " [Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indies "Indies")" refers to the land east of [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River "Indus River"). It is fully interchangeable with the word India. The Portuguese initially described the entire region they discovered as the Indies. Caribbean islands were initially described as "Indies" as they were thought to be India. When they became known to be in [Western Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Hemisphere "Western Hemisphere"), they were renamed as [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies "West Indies"). Thus, West Indies means India in the Western Hemisphere. [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia")'s former name is [Dutch East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies "Dutch East Indies") which means India in [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia") (this should not be confused with [Dutch India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_India "Dutch India"), which referred to the trading posts of [VOC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company "Dutch East India Company") within the Indian subcontinent).

## Bharat

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Hathigumpha_inscription_%282020%29.jpg/500px-Hathigumpha_inscription_%282020%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hathigumpha_inscription_(2020).jpg) [Hathigumpha Jain Inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hathigumpha_inscription "Hathigumpha inscription") of King [Kharavela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharavela "Kharavela") (2nd century BCE), which contains the earliest known epigraphical reference to "Bharat".[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Bharatha.jpg/500px-Bharatha.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bharatha.jpg) An ancient idol of [Bharata Chakravarti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharata_(Jainism) "Bharata (Jainism)") at [Shravanabelagola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shravanabelagola "Shravanabelagola") Jain temple, near the resting site [Chandragupta Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Maurya "Chandragupta Maurya").

Bharat is a coequal name of India, as set down in [Article 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part_I_of_the_Constitution_of_India "Part I of the Constitution of India") of the Constitution, adopted in 1950, which states in English: "India, that is Bharat,..."[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-Clementin-Ojha-25) Bharat, which was predominantly used in Sanskrit, was adopted as a self-ascribed alternative name by some people of the Indian subcontinent and the Republic of India.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-26)

The Sanskrit word _Bhārata_ is a [vrddhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrddhi "Vrddhi") derivation of _Bharata_, which was originally an epithet of [Agni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agni "Agni"). The term is a verbal noun of the Sanskrit root _bhr-_, "to bear/to carry", with a literal meaning of to be maintained (of [fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire "Fire")). The [root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_(linguistics) "Root (linguistics)") _bhr-_ is cognate with the English verb 'to bear' and Latin _ferō_. This term also means "one who is engaged in search for knowledge". _Barato_, the [Esperanto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esperanto "Esperanto") name for India, is also a derivation of _Bhārata_.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWitzel199919-27)[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-28)

According to Vedic texts, Bharat is derived from the name of the [Vedic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_period "Vedic period") community [Bharatas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatas_(Vedic_tribe) "Bharatas (Vedic tribe)"), who are mentioned in the [Rigveda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigveda "Rigveda") as one of the original community of the [Āryāvarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80ry%C4%81varta "Āryāvarta") and notably participating in the [Battle of the Ten Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Ten_Kings "Battle of the Ten Kings").[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-29)[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-30)[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-Scharfe-31)[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-32)

In the [ancient Indian epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_epic_poetry "Indian epic poetry"), the _[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata "Mahabharata")_ (200 BCE to 300 CE), a larger region of [Indosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indosphere "Indosphere") is encompassed by the term Bharat.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-33) Some other [Puranic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puranas "Puranas") passages refer to the same Bhārata people, who are described as the descendants of [Dushyanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dushyanta "Dushyanta")'s son [Bharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharata_(Mahabharata) "Bharata (Mahabharata)") in the _Mahabharata_.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-34)

In the Puranas refer to the land that comprises India as _Bhāratavarṣa_ (lit.'Bharat mainland') and uses this term to distinguish it from other _varṣa_s or continents.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-pargiter-35) For example, the _[Vayu Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vayu_Purana "Vayu Purana")_ says "he who conquers the whole of Bhāratavarṣa is celebrated as a samrāṭa".[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-pargiter_p.40-36) According to [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism") tradition, _Bhāratavarṣa_ in the Puranas – is named after [Emperor Bharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharata_(Jainism) "Bharata (Jainism)"), the son of [Rishabhdeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishabhdeva "Rishabhdeva") the first [Tirthankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirthankara "Tirthankara") of Jainism. He is described to be a [Kshatriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kshatriya "Kshatriya") born in the [Solar dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_dynasty "Solar dynasty").[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-37) The earliest epigraphical record of Bhāratavarṣa in a geographical sense is in the [Hathigumpha inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hathigumpha_inscription "Hathigumpha inscription") of Jain king [Kharavela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharavela "Kharavela") (first century BCE), where it applies only to a restrained area of northern India, namely the part of the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges "Ganges") west of [Magadha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadha_(Mahajanapada) "Magadha (Mahajanapada)"). The inscription also mentions that Bharat was named after Emperor Bharata, the son of Rishabhdeva.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-Jha-4)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-Upinder_Singh-5)

_[Vishnu Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu_Purana "Vishnu Purana")_ (2,3,1), _Vayu Purana_ (33,52), _[Linga Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linga_Purana "Linga Purana")_ (1,47,23), _[Brahmanda Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmanda_Purana "Brahmanda Purana")_ (14,5,62), _[Agni Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agni_Purana "Agni Purana")_ (107,11–12), _[Skanda Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skanda_Purana "Skanda Purana")_ (37,57) and _[Markandeya Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markandeya_Purana "Markandeya Purana")_ (50,41), all use the designation _Bhāratavarṣa_.

The _Vishnu Purana_ mentions:

> Uttaraṃ yat samudrasya himādreścaiva dakṣiṇam.
>
> varṣaṃ tad bhārataṃ nāma bhāratī yatra santatiḥ.

—Vishnu Purana (2,3,1)

Translation:

> The country that lies north of the ocean and south of the snowy mountains is called Bhāratam there dwell the descendants of Bharata.

The _Bhagavata Purana_ mentions (Canto 5, Chapter 4)[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-38) – "He (Rishabhdeva) begot a hundred sons that were exactly like him... He (Bharata) had the best qualities and it was because of him that this land by the people is called Bhāratavarṣa."[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-39)

Bharata Khanda (or Bhārata Kṣētra)[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-dikshit-40) is a term used in some of the [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain_literature "Jain literature") and [Hindu texts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_texts "Hindu texts").[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-dikshit-40)

The use of Bharat often has political overtones, appealing to [Hindu nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_nationalism "Hindu nationalism").[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-41) In 2023, Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narendra_Modi "Narendra Modi")'s [Hindu nationalist government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premiership_of_Narendra_Modi "Premiership of Narendra Modi") used the name Bharat in connection with a [G20 gathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_G20_New_Delhi_summit "2023 G20 New Delhi summit"), which caused speculation on a name-change for the country.[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-CNN_20_September_2023-42)[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-43) Such a change would need a constitutional amendment, meaning two-thirds of the vote in each of the two houses of parliament,[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-44) and an official notice to the UN, advising how to write the name in the UN's [six official languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_languages_of_the_United_Nations "Official languages of the United Nations").[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-BBC_28_November_2023-45)

The name " [al-Hind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Hind "Al-Hind")" (here [بالهند](https://ary.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%87%D9%86%D8%AF "ary:بالهند") _Bil'Hind_, "In India") on an Umayyad coin minted in India, from the time of the first [Governor of Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphal_province_of_Sind "Caliphal province of Sind") [Muhammad ibn Qasim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_ibn_Qasim "Muhammad ibn Qasim") in 715 CE.[\[a\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-49)

In [Middle Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Persian "Middle Persian"), probably from the first century CE, the suffix _-stān_ ( [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language"): ستان) was added, indicative of a country or region, forming the name _Hindūstān_.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-50) Thus, Sindh was referred to as _Hindustān_ in the Naqsh-e-Rustam inscription of [Sassanid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanid "Sassanid") [emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_monarchs_of_the_Sasanian_Empire "List of monarchs of the Sasanian Empire") [Shapur I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shapur_I "Shapur I") in c. 262 CE.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-51)[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-52)

[Emperor Babur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur "Babur") of the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire "Mughal Empire") said, "On the East, the South, and the West it is bounded by the Great Ocean."[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-53) _Hind_ was notably adapted in the [Arabic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language "Arabic language") as the definitive form _Al-Hind_ (الهند) for India, for example, in the 11th-century _Tarikh Al-Hind_ ('History of India'). It occurs intermittently in usage within India, such as in the phrase _[Jai Hind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jai_Hind "Jai Hind")_ ( [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi_language "Hindi language"): जय हिन्द) or in _Hind Mahāsāgar_ (हिन्द महासागर), the standard Hindi name for the [Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean "Indian Ocean").

Both the names were current in [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language") and [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic "Arabic"), and from that into northern Indian languages, from the 11th century [Islamic conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_in_the_Indian_subcontinent "Muslim conquest in the Indian subcontinent"): the rulers in the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate "Delhi Sultanate") and Mughal periods called their Indian dominion, centered around [Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi "Delhi"), "Hindustan". In contemporary Persian and [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi "Hindi")- [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu "Urdu"), the term Hindustan has recently come to mean the Republic of India. The same is the case with Arabic, where _al-Hind_ is the name for the Republic of India.

"Hindustan", as the term [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu "Hindu") itself, entered the English language in the 17th century. In the 19th century, the term as used in English referred to the subcontinent. "Hindustan" was in use simultaneously with "India" during the [British era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_rule "British rule").

## Jambudvīpa

Main article: [Jambudvīpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jambudv%C4%ABpa "Jambudvīpa")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/08/Jambudiipasi.jpg/500px-Jambudiipasi.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Jambudiipasi.jpg) The name _Jambudīpasi_ for "India" ( [Brahmi script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmi_script "Brahmi script")) in the Sahasram [Minor Rock Edict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_Rock_Edict "Minor Rock Edict") of [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka "Ashoka"), circa 250 BCE.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-54)

[Jambudvīpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jambudv%C4%ABpa "Jambudvīpa") ( [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_language "Sanskrit language"): जम्बुद्वीपम्, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Sanskrit "Romanization of Sanskrit"):_Jambu-dvīpam_, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation "Literal translation")'berry island') was used in ancient scriptures as a name of India before the term _Bhārat_ became widespread. It might be an indirect reference to the [Insular India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insular_India "Insular India").\[ _[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")_\] In [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar") it was called "Zabudipa".[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-bh-29-55) The derivative _Jambu Dwipa_ was the historical term for India in many [Southeast Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia") countries before the introduction of the English word "India". This alternate name is still used occasionally in [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand"), [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia"), [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java "Java") and [Bali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bali "Bali") to describe the Indian subcontinent. However, it also can refer to the whole continent of Asia. It was used by Maurya Emperor [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka "Ashoka") in his inscriptions to denote his realm.

## Gyagar and Phagyul

Both Gyagar ("White expanse") and Phagyul are [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Tibetan "Classical Tibetan") names for India. Ancient [Tibetan Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism "Tibetan Buddhism") authors and pilgrims used the ethnogeographic referents _Gyagar_ or _Gyagar to the south_ and _Madhyadesa_ ( _central land_ or holy centre) for India. Since at least the 13th century, several influential indigenous [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people "Tibetan people") [lamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lama "Lama") and authors referred to India as _Phagyul_, short for _Phags yul_, meaning _the land of [Aryas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_peoples "Indo-Aryan peoples"),_ referring to noble or spiritually enlightened people regarded in Tibetan Buddhism as the source of Buddhist learning.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-tib1-56) Tibetan scholar [Gendun Chopel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gend%C3%BCn_Ch%C3%B6phel "Gendün Chöphel") explains that the Tibetan word G _yagar_ derives from the [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_language "Sanskrit language") _[vihāra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vih%C4%81ra "Vihāra")_ (Buddhist monastery), and the ancient Tibetans applied the term _Geysar_ mainly to the northern and central India region from [Kuru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuru_Kingdom "Kuru Kingdom") (modern [Haryana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haryana "Haryana")) to [Magadha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadha_(Mahajanapada) "Magadha (Mahajanapada)") (modern [Bihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bihar "Bihar")).[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-tib4-57) The _[Epic of King Gesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_of_King_Gesar "Epic of King Gesar")_, which developed between 200 BCE-300 BCE and about 600 CE, describes India as " _Gyagar: The Kingdom of Buddhist Doctrine_", " _Gyagar: The Kingdom of Aru Medicine_" ( [Ayurveda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayurveda "Ayurveda")), " _Gyagar: [The Kingdom of Pearls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Fishery_Coast "Pearl Fishery Coast")_", and " _Gyagar: [The Kingdom of Golden Vases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatti_Gold_Mines#History "Hatti Gold Mines")_".[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-tib2-58) The [Central Tibetan Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Tibetan_Administration "Central Tibetan Administration"), often referred to as the Tibetan Government-in-Exile, asserts "Tibet is inextricably The [Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalai_Lama "Dalai Lama") has described India as Tibet's [guru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru "Guru") and Tibet as its [chela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru%E2%80%93shishya_tradition "Guru–shishya tradition") (disciple), referred to himself as a 'Son of India' and a true follower of [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi "Mahatma Gandhi"). He continues to advocate the revival of India's ancient wisdom based on the [Nalanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalanda "Nalanda") tradition."[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-tib3-59)

## Tianzhu

Main article: [Tianzhu (India)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianzhu_(India) "Tianzhu (India)")

_Tianzhu_ (天竺,[OC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Chinese "Old Chinese"):[\*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_of_Old_Chinese "Reconstruction of Old Chinese") ‍qʰl'iːntuɡ, [mod.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Mandarin "Standard Mandarin") _Tiānzhú_) is one of several Chinese transliterations of the Sanskrit _Sindhu_ via Persian _Hindu_[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-cheung-60) and is used since ancient times in China and its peripheries. Its [Sino-Xenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Xenic "Sino-Xenic") reading is _Tenjiku_ in Japanese, _Cheonchuk_ (천축) in [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language "Korean language"), and _Thiên Trúc_ in [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_language "Vietnamese language"). Devout Buddhists in the [Sinosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinosphere "Sinosphere") traditionally used this term and its related forms to designate India as their "heavenly centre", referring to the sacred origins of [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism") in the Indian subcontinent.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-61)[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-62)

Other forms include _Juandu_ ([身毒](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E8%BA%AB%E6%AF%92 "wikt:身毒"); _Juāndú_), which appears in [Sima Qian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sima_Qian "Sima Qian")'s [Shiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Records_of_the_Grand_Historian "Records of the Grand Historian"). Another is _Tiandu_ (天篤; _Tiāndǔ_), which is used in the _[Hou Hanshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hou_Hanshu "Hou Hanshu")_ (Book of the Later Han).[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-yu-63)_Yintejia or Indəkka_ (印特伽; _Yìntèjiā_) comes from the [Kuchean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuchean_language "Kuchean language") _Indaka_, another transliteration of _Hindu_.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-cheung-60)

A detailed account of Tianzhu is given in the "Xiyu Zhuan" (Record of the Western Regions) in the _Hou Hanshu_ compiled by [Fan Ye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fan_Ye_(historian) "Fan Ye (historian)") (398–445):

> The state of Tianzhu: Also named Shendu, it lies several thousand _li_ southeast of [Yuezhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuezhi "Yuezhi"). Its customs are the same as those of Yuezhi, and it is low, damp, and very hot. It borders a large river. The inhabitants ride on elephants in warfare; they are weaker than the Yuezhi. They practise the way of Futu (the [Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddha "Buddha")), \[and therefore\] it has become a custom among them not to kill or attack \[others\]. From west of the states Yuezhi and Gaofu, and south until the Western Sea, and east until the state of Panqi, all is the territory of Shendu. Shendu has several hundred separate towns, with a governor, and separate states which can be numbered in the tens, each with its own king. Although there are small differences among them, they all come under the general name of Shendu, and at this time all are subject to Yuezhi. Yuezhi have killed their kings and established a general in order to rule over their people. The land produces [elephants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephants "Elephants"), rhinoceros, tortoise shell, [gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold "Gold"), [silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver "Silver"), copper, iron, lead, and tin. It communicates to the west with [Da Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Da_Qin "Da Qin") and (so) has the exotica of Da Qin.[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-yu-63)

Tianzhu was also referred to as _Wu Tianzhu_ (五天竺; _Wǔ Tiānzhú_; 'Five Indias'), because there were five geographical regions in India known to the Chinese: Central, Eastern, Western, Northern, and Southern India. The monk [Xuanzang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang "Xuanzang") also referred to India as _Wu Yin_ (五印; _Wǔ Yìn_; 'Five Inds').[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-cheung-60)

The name _Tianzhu_ and its Sino-Xenic cognates were eventually replaced by terms derived from the [Middle Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Chinese "Middle Chinese") borrowing of \* _yentu_ from Kuchean, though a very long time elapsed between that term's first use and its becoming the standard modern name for India in East Asian languages. Pronounced _Yindu_ ( [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"): 印度; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin "Pinyin"): _Yìndù_) in Chinese, it was first used by the seventh-century monk and traveler [Xuanzang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang "Xuanzang").[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-64) In Japanese for example, the name _Indo_ (インド, 印度, or occasionally 印土) had been found occasionally in 18th and early 19th-century works, such as [Arai Hakuseki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arai_Hakuseki "Arai Hakuseki")'s _[Sairan Igen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sairan_Igen "Sairan Igen")_ (1713) and [Yamamura Saisuke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamamura_Saisuke?action=edit&redlink=1 "Yamamura Saisuke")\[ [ja](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%B1%B1%E6%9D%91%E6%89%8D%E5%8A%A9 "ja:山村才助")\]'s _Indoshi_ (印度志, a translation of a work by [Johann Hübner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_H%C3%BCbner "Johann Hübner")). However, the use of the name _Tenjiku_, which was heavily associated with the image of [India as a land of Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Buddhism_in_India "History of Buddhism in India"), was not completely displaced until the early 20th century: scholars such as [Soyen Shaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyen_Shaku "Soyen Shaku") and [Seki Seisetsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seki_Seisetsu?action=edit&redlink=1 "Seki Seisetsu")\[ [ja](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%96%A2%E7%B2%BE%E6%8B%99 "ja:関精拙")\] who travelled to India for pilgrimages to Buddhist historical sites, continued to use the name _Tenjiku_ to emphasise the religious aspect of their travels, though most of their contemporaries (even fellow Buddhist pilgrims) adopted the name _Indo_ by then.[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-65)[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-66)

## Hodu

Hodu ( [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language"): הֹדּוּ _Hodû_) is the [Biblical Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_Hebrew "Biblical Hebrew") name for India mentioned in the _[Book of Esther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Esther "Book of Esther")_ part of the Jewish [Tanakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanakh "Tanakh") and Christian [Old Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Testament "Old Testament"). [Ahasuerus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahasuerus "Ahasuerus") was described as King ruling 127 provinces from _Hodu_ (India) to _[Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia "Ethiopia")_.[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-67) The term seemingly derives from [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit") _Sindhu_, "great river", i.e., the [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River "Indus River"), via [Old Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian "Old Persian") _Hiñd°u_.[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_India#cite_note-68)
