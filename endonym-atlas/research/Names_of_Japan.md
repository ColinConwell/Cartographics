The word _[Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan")_ is an [exonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endonym_and_exonym "Endonym and exonym"), and is used (in one form or another) by many languages. The [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language") names for Japan are _**Nihon**_ ([\[ɲi.hoꜜɴ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Japanese "Help:IPA/Japanese")[Play audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/92/Ja-nihon%28%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%29.ogg/Ja-nihon%28%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%29.ogg.mp3 "Play audio")[ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ja-nihon(%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC).ogg "File:Ja-nihon(日本).ogg"), [hiragana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiragana "Hiragana"): にほん) and _**Nippon**_ ([\[ɲip.poꜜɴ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Japanese "Help:IPA/Japanese")[Play audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/db/Ja-nippon%28%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%29.ogg/Ja-nippon%28%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%29.ogg.mp3 "Play audio")[ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ja-nippon(%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC).ogg "File:Ja-nippon(日本).ogg") hiragana: にっぽん). They are both written in Japanese using the [kanji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanji "Kanji") 日本.

During the early imperial period in China, Japan was known as _[Wa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wa_(name_of_Japan) "Wa (name of Japan)")_ (倭) or _Wa-koku_ (倭國, literally "Wa-nation")[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-1), which can mean either "dwarf" or "submissive".[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-carr-2):4–6 Japanese scribes found fault with its [offensive connotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic_pejoratives_in_written_Chinese "Graphic pejoratives in written Chinese"), and officially changed the characters they used to spell the native name for Japan, then known as _Yamato_, replacing the 倭 ("dwarf") character _Wa_ with the homophone 和 ("peaceful, harmonious"). _Wa_ 和 was often combined with 大 ("great") to form the name 大和, which is read as _Yamato_[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-fuzita-4) (see also [Jukujikun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jukujikun "Jukujikun") for a discussion of this type of spelling where the kanji and pronunciations are not directly related). The earliest record of _Nippon_ (日本) appears in the Chinese _[Old Book of Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Book_of_Tang "Old Book of Tang")_, which notes the change in 703 when Japanese envoys requested that its name be changed. It is believed that the name change within Japan itself took place sometime between 665 and 703,[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-5) during the [Heian period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heian_period "Heian period"), _Yamato_ (大和) was gradually replaced by _Nippon_ (日本). In 1076, Turkic scholar [Mahmud al-Kashgari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_al-Kashgari "Mahmud al-Kashgari") in his book [Dīwān Lughāt al-Turk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C4%ABw%C4%81n_Lugh%C4%81t_al-Turk "Dīwān Lughāt al-Turk") mentioned this country as 'Jabarqa' (جَابَرْقَا).[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-6) [Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo "Marco Polo") called Japan 'Cipangu' around 1300, based on the Chinese enunciation of the name,[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-marco-polo-cipangu-7) probably 日本國; 'sun source country' (compare modern [Min Nan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Nan "Min Nan") pronunciation _ji̍t pún kok_). In the 16th century in [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca "Malacca"), [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people "Portuguese people") traders first heard from [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_language "Malaysian language") the names _Jepang_, _Jipang_, and _Jepun_.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-trauay-8) In 1577 it was first recorded in English, spelled _Giapan_.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-trauay-8) At the end of the 16th century, Portuguese [missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary "Missionary") came to coastal islands of Japan and created brief grammars and dictionaries of [Middle Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Middle_Japanese "Late Middle Japanese") for the purpose of trade. The 1603–1604 dictionary [Vocabvlario da Lingoa de Iapam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippo_Jisho "Nippo Jisho") has 2 entries: _nifon_[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-Doi_1980:463-9) and _iippon_.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-Doi_1980:363-10) Since then many derived names of Japan appeared on early-modern European maps.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/FramauroJava.jpg/250px-FramauroJava.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:FramauroJava.jpg) Cipangu (cited as _ixola de cimpagu_ on the center-left) on the 1453 [Fra Mauro map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fra_Mauro_map "Fra Mauro map"), the first known Western depiction of the island

Both _Nippon_ and _Nihon_ literally mean "the sun's origin", that is, where the sun originates,[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-nussbaum707-11) and are often translated as the _Land of the Rising Sun_. This nomenclature comes from [Imperial correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_embassies_to_China "Imperial embassies to China") with the [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") [Sui dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_dynasty "Sui dynasty") and refers to Japan's eastern position relative to [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"). Before _Nihon_ came into official use, Japan was known as _[Wa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wa_(name_of_Japan) "Wa (name of Japan)")_ (倭) or _Wakoku_ (倭国).[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-12) _Wa_ was a name early China used to refer to an ethnic group living in [Japan around the time of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yayoi_period "Yayoi period") the [Three Kingdoms period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms "Three Kingdoms").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

### Wa

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Names_of_Japan&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Wa")\]

Although the etymological origins of "Wa" remain uncertain, Chinese historical texts recorded an ancient people residing in the Japanese archipelago (perhaps Kyūshū), named something like \*ˀWâ or \*ˀWər 倭. Carr (1992:9–10) surveys prevalent proposals for Wa's etymology ranging from feasible (transcribing Japanese first-person pronouns _waga_ 我が "my; our" and _ware_ 我 "I; oneself; thou") to shameful (writing Japanese _Wa_ as 倭 implying "dwarf"), and summarizes interpretations for \*ˀWâ "Japanese" into variations on two etymologies: "behaviorally 'submissive' or physically 'short'." The first "submissive; obedient" explanation began with the (121 CE) _[Shuowen Jiezi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuowen_Jiezi "Shuowen Jiezi")_ dictionary. It defines 倭 as _shùnmào_ 順皃 "obedient/submissive/docile appearance", graphically explains the "person; human" radical 亻 with a _wěi_ 委 "bent" phonetic, and quotes the above _[Shijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shijing "Shijing")_ poem.\[ _[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")_\] "Conceivably, when Chinese first met Japanese," Carr (1992:9) suggests "they transcribed Wa as \*ˀWâ 'bent back' signifying 'compliant' bowing/obeisance. Bowing is noted in early historical references to Japan." Examples include "Respect is shown by squatting" ( _Hou Han Shu_, tr. Tsunoda 1951:2), and "they either squat or kneel, with both hands on the ground. This is the way they show respect." (Wei Zhi, tr. Tsunoda 1951:13). Koji Nakayama interprets _wēi_ 逶 "winding" as "very far away" and euphemistically translates _Wō_ 倭 as "separated from the continent." The second etymology of _wō_ 倭 meaning "dwarf, pygmy" has possible cognates in _ǎi_ 矮 "low, short (of stature)", _wō_ 踒 "strain; sprain; bent legs", and _wò_ 臥 "lie down; crouch; sit (animals and birds)". Early Chinese dynastic histories refer to a _Zhūrúguó_ 侏儒國 "pygmy/dwarf country" located south of Japan, associated with possibly Okinawa Island or the Ryukyu Islands. Carr cites the historical precedence of construing Wa as "submissive people" and the "Country of Dwarfs" legend as evidence that the "little people" etymology was a secondary development.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-carr-2):9

Chinese, Korean, and Japanese scribes regularly wrote _Wa_ or _Yamato_ "Japan" with the Chinese character 倭 until the 8th century, when the Japanese found fault with it due to its [offensive connotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic_pejoratives_in_written_Chinese "Graphic pejoratives in written Chinese"), replacing it with 和 "harmony, peace, balance". Retroactively, this character was adopted in Japan to refer to the country itself, often combined with the character 大 (literally meaning "Great"), so as to write the name as _Yamato_ (大和) (Great Wa, in a manner similar to _e.g._ 大清帝國 [Great Qing Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Qing_Empire "Great Qing Empire"), 大英帝國 [Empire of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire")). However, the pronunciation _Yamato_ cannot be formed from the sounds of its constituent characters; it refers to a place in Japan and, based on [the specific spellings used in ancient documents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Ddai_Tokushu_Kanazukai "Jōdai Tokushu Kanazukai") (see also [Man'yōgana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man'y%C5%8Dgana "Man'yōgana") and [Old Japanese#Vowels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Japanese#Vowels "Old Japanese")), this may have originally meant "Mountain Place" (山処).[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-13)[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-14)[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-KDJ-15) Such words which use certain kanji to name a certain Japanese word solely for the purpose of representing the word's meaning regardless of the given kanji's [on'yomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On'yomi "On'yomi") or [kun'yomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kun'yomi "Kun'yomi"), a.k.a. [jukujikun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jukujikun "Jukujikun"), is not uncommon in Japanese. Other original names in Chinese texts include _[Yamatai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamatai "Yamatai") country_ (邪馬台国), where a [Queen Himiko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himiko "Himiko") lived. When _hi no moto_, the indigenous Japanese way of saying "sun's origin", was written in [kanji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanji "Kanji"), it was given the characters 日本.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] In time, these characters began to be read using [Sino-Japanese readings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On'yomi "On'yomi"), first _Nippon_ and later _Nihon,_ although the two names are interchangeable to this day.

### Nippon

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Names_of_Japan&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Nippon")\]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/97/Excerpt_of_Yegun%27s_Epitaph.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Excerpt_of_Yegun's_Epitaph.png) Excerpt of Yegun's Epitaph

The very first historically attested record of _Nippon_ (日本) is found in an epitaph of a [Paekche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paekche "Paekche") individual called [Yegun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yegun?action=edit&redlink=1 "Yegun (page does not exist)")\[ [ko](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/%EC%98%88%EA%B5%B0 "ko:예군")\] (禰軍; 예군), believed to have been created around 678 that was discovered in 2011.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-:7-16)[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-:8-17) The epitaph engraves the aftermath of the [Battle of Baekgang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Baekgang "Battle of Baekgang") where [Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silla "Silla") and [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty "Tang dynasty") alliance invaded Paekche and many of the refugees fled to Japan as [Toraijins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toraijin "Toraijin").

> 『日本餘噍 據扶桑以逋誅』
>
> * * *
>
> "The refugees of Ilbon (日本; referring to [Paekche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paekche "Paekche")) safely retreated with the help of [Busang Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamato_Kingship "Yamato Kingship") (扶桑; referring to Japan) from the invaders ( [Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silla "Silla")– [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty "Tang dynasty") alliance)."

— _Excerpt from "Yegun's Epithaph"_, 678 CE

According to Chinese historian Lianlong Wang (王連龍), the characters of _Nippon_ are referring to the kingdom of Paekche as Japan was referred to as Busang Kingdom (扶桑國).[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-:7-16) Historically, "Busang Kingdom (also known as " [Fusang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusang "Fusang")" in Chinese)" was reserved for the "farthest east kingdom" depending on the nation that used it (i.e. Korea was a "Fusang Kingdom" from China's perspective) similar to [Cheonggu/Qingqiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheonggu "Cheonggu"). However, since the individual was Korean, "Busang Kingdom" in the context of his epitaph was directing it towards Japan (then [Yamato Kingship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamato_Kingship "Yamato Kingship")) as the kingdom of Japan was farthest east from Korea's perspective, while _Nippon_ was dedicated to Paekche, his home nation.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-:7-16) Japanese historian Haruyuki Tono (東野 治之) also made a similar conclusion, stating that the term _Nippon_ originally meant "Paekche" before being adopted by the Japanese[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-:8-17) most likely due to the influence from the Paekche Toraijins.

_Nippon_ also appeared in history only at the end of the 7th century. The _[Old Book of Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Book_of_Tang "Old Book of Tang")_ (舊唐書; 945), one of the [Twenty-Four Histories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-Four_Histories "Twenty-Four Histories"), also stated that the Japanese envoy disliked his country's name _[Woguo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woguo "Woguo")_ (Chinese) (倭國), and changed it to _Nippon_ (日本), or "Origin of the Sun". Another 8th-century chronicle, _True Meaning of Shiji_ (史記正義), however, states that the first female Chinese Emperor [Wu Zetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Zetian "Wu Zetian") ordered a Japanese envoy to change the country's name to _Nippon_. It has been suggested that the name change in Japan may have taken place sometime between 665 and 703, and Wu Zetian then acceded to the name change in China following a request from a delegation from Japan in 703.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-18) The sun plays an important role in [Japanese mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_mythology "Japanese mythology") and [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto "Shinto") as the [emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_of_Japan "Emperor of Japan") is said to be the [direct descendant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_imperial_family_tree "Japanese imperial family tree") of the sun goddess [Amaterasu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaterasu "Amaterasu") and the legitimacy of the [ruling house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_House_of_Japan "Imperial House of Japan") rested on this divine appointment and [descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_imperial_family_tree "Japanese imperial family tree") from the chief deity of the predominant [Shinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto "Shinto") religion. The name of the country reflects this central importance of the sun. The association of the country with the sun was indicated in a letter sent in 607 and recorded in the [official history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Sui "Book of Sui") of the [Sui dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_dynasty "Sui dynasty"). [Prince Shōtoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Sh%C5%8Dtoku "Prince Shōtoku"), the Regent of Japan, sent a mission to China with a letter in which he called the emperor of Japan (actually [an empress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_Suiko "Empress Suiko") at the time) "the [Son of Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_of_Heaven "Son of Heaven") of the Land where the Sun rises" (日出處天子). The message said: "The Son of Heaven, on the Land of the Rising Sun, sends this letter to the Son of Heaven of the Land, where the Sun sets, and wishes him well".[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-19)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-20)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/Martin_Behaim_1492_Ocean_Map.png/250px-Martin_Behaim_1492_Ocean_Map.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Martin_Behaim_1492_Ocean_Map.png) _Cipangu_ described on the 1492 [Martin Behaim globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdapfel "Erdapfel")

The English word for Japan came to the West from early trade routes. The early [Mandarin Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese "Mandarin Chinese") or possibly [Wu Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Chinese "Wu Chinese") word for Japan was recorded by [Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo "Marco Polo") as _Cipangu_.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-marco-polo-cipangu-7) The [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_language "Malaysian language") and [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language "Indonesian language") words _Jepang_, _Jipang_, and _Jepun_ were borrowed from non-Mandarin Chinese languages, and this Malay word was encountered by [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal "Portugal") traders in [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca "Malacca") in the 16th century. It is thought the Portuguese traders were the first to bring the word to [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe "Europe"). It was first recorded in English in 1577 spelled _Giapan_.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-trauay-8)

In English, the modern official title of the country is simply "Japan", one of the few countries to have no " [long form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states "List of sovereign states")" name. The official Japanese-language name is _Nippon-koku_ or _Nihon-koku_ (日本国), literally " _[Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_state "Nation state") of Japan_".[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-21) As an adjective, the term "Dai-Nippon" remains popular with Japanese governmental, commercial, or social organizations whose reach extend beyond Japan's geographic borders (e.g., [Dai Nippon Printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dai_Nippon_Printing "Dai Nippon Printing"), [Dai Nippon Butoku Kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dai_Nippon_Butoku_Kai "Dai Nippon Butoku Kai"), etc.).

Though _Nippon_ or _Nihon_ are still by far the most popular names for Japan from within the country, recently the foreign words _Japan_ and even _Jipangu_ (from _Cipangu_, see below) have been used in Japanese mostly for the purpose of [foreign branding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_branding "Foreign branding").

## Phonology

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Names_of_Japan&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Phonology")\]

[Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people "Portuguese people") [missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary "Missionary") arrived in Japan at the end of the 16th century. In the course of learning [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language"), they created several grammars and dictionaries of [Middle Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Middle_Japanese "Late Middle Japanese"). The 1603–1604 dictionary [Vocabvlario da Lingoa de Iapam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippo_Jisho "Nippo Jisho") contains two entries for Japan: _nifon_[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-Doi_1980:463-9) and _iippon_.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-Doi_1980:363-10)
The title of the dictionary ( _Vocabulary of the Language of Japan_) illustrates that the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language "Portuguese language") word for Japan was by that time _Iapam_.

### Nifon

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Names_of_Japan&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Nifon")\]

Historically, Japanese /h/ has undergone a number of phonological changes. Originally \*\[ [p](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiceless_bilabial_plosive "Voiceless bilabial plosive")\], this weakened into \[ [ɸ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiceless_bilabial_fricative "Voiceless bilabial fricative")\] and eventually became the modern \[ [h](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiceless_glottal_fricative "Voiceless glottal fricative")\]. Modern /h/ is still pronounced \[ɸ\] when followed by /ɯ/.

Middle Japanese _nifon_ becomes Modern Japanese _nihon_ via regular phonological changes.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

### Jippon

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Names_of_Japan&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Jippon")\]

Before modern styles of [romanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Japanese "Romanization of Japanese"), the Portuguese devised their [own](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Japanese#Historical_romanizations "Romanization of Japanese"). In it, /zi/ is written as either _ii_ or _ji_. In modern [Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepburn_romanization "Hepburn romanization") style, _iippon_ would be rendered as _Jippon_. There are no historical phonological changes to take into account here.

Etymologically, _Jippon_ is similar to _Nippon_ in that it is an alternative reading of 日本. The initial character 日 may also be read as /ziti/ or /zitu/. Compounded with /hoɴ/ (本), this regularly becomes _Jippon_.

Unlike the _Nihon_/ _Nippon_ doublet, there is no evidence for a \* _Jihon_.

## Nihon and Nippon

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Names_of_Japan&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Nihon and Nippon")\]

The [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language") name for Japan, 日本, can be pronounced either _Nihon_ or _Nippon_. Both readings come from the [on'yomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On'yomi "On'yomi").

### Meaning

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Names_of_Japan&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Meaning")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/9e/Flag_of_Japan.svg/250px-Flag_of_Japan.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Flag_of_Japan.svg) The national flag of Japan is a rectangular white banner with a red circle at its center. The flag is officially called the _Nisshōki_ (日章旗, 'flag of the sun') but is more commonly known in Japan as the _Hinomaru_ (日の丸, 'circle of the sun'). It embodies the country's sobriquet: the Land of the Rising Sun.

日 ( _nichi_) means "sun" or "day"; 本 ( _hon_) means "base" or "origin". The compound means "origin of the sun", or "source of the sun"[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-r864-22) or "where the sun rises" (from a westbound point of view—which includes lands west of the nation, the sun rises from Japan); it is a source for the popular Western description of Japan as the "Land of the Rising Sun".

_Nichi_, in compounds, often loses the final _chi_ and creates a slight pause between the first and second syllables of the compound. When romanised, this pause is represented by a [doubling of the first consonant of the second syllable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokuon "Sokuon"); thus _nichi_ 日 plus _kō_ 光 (light) is written and pronounced _nikkō_, meaning sunlight.

Latest by the [Meiji era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_era "Meiji era") and the promulgation of the first modern constitution the official name was " [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan")" _Dai Nippon Teikoku_ (大日本帝国),[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-Shillony-23) which translates to "Empire of Great Japan" ( [_Dai_](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A7#Japanese "wikt:大") "Great", [_Nippon_](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC#Japanese "wikt:日本") "Japanese", [_Teikoku_](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%B8%9D%E5%9B%BD#Japanese "wikt:帝国") "Empire"). Due to its name in _[kanji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanji "Kanji")_ characters and its flag, it was also given the [exonyms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exonym "Exonym") " [Empire of the Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_the_Sun "Empire of the Sun")" and "Empire of the Rising Sun".

### Evolution

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Names_of_Japan&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Evolution")\]

Japanese [日](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%97%A5 "wiktionary:日") and [本](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%9C%AC "wiktionary:本") were historically pronounced _niti_ (or _jitu_, reflecting a [Late Middle Chinese pronunciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kan-on "Kan-on")) and _pon_, respectively. In compounds, however, final voiceless stops (i.e. _p_, _t_, _k_) of the first word were unreleased in Middle Chinese, and the pronunciation of 日本 was thus _Nippon_ or _Jippon_ (with the adjacent consonants assimilating).

[Min Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Chinese "Min Chinese") languages still retain this pronunciation of 日本, such as [Northern Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Min "Northern Min") _Nì-bǒ̤ng_ ( [Jian'ou dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jian'ou_dialect "Jian'ou dialect")) or [Fuzhounese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou_language "Fuzhou language") _Nĭk-buōng_. In modern [Toisanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taishanese "Taishanese"), a [Yue Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yue_Chinese "Yue Chinese") language, 日本 is pronounced as _[Ngìp Bāwn](https://www.stephen-li.com/TaishaneseVocabulary/Misc2/Audio/misc2%20Part%20081%20of%20214.mp3)_ [\[ŋip˦˨ bɔn˥\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA "Help:IPA").[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-24)

Historical sound change in Japanese has led to the modern pronunciations of the individual characters as _nichi_ and _hon_. The pronunciation _Nihon_ originated, possibly in the [Kantō region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kant%C5%8D_region "Kantō region"), as a reintroduction of this independent pronunciation of 本 into the compound. This must have taken place during the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period "Edo period"), after [another sound change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Middle_Japanese#Phonology "Late Middle Japanese") occurred which would have resulted in this form becoming _Niwon_ and later _Nion_.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

Several attempts to choose a definitive official reading were rejected by the Japanese government, which declared both to be correct.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-25)

### Modern

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Names_of_Japan&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Modern")\]

While both pronunciations are correct, _Nippon_ is frequently preferred for official purposes,[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-26) including [money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yen "Yen"), [stamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postage_stamp "Postage stamp"), and international [sporting events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporting_event "Sporting event"), as well as the _Nippon-koku_, literally the " _State of Japan_" (日本国).

Other than this, there seem to be no fixed rules for choosing one pronunciation over the other, but in some cases, one form is simply more common. For example, Japanese-speakers generally call their language _[Nihongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language")_; _Nippongo_, while possible,[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-27)
is rarely used. In other cases, uses are variable. The name for the [Bank of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Japan "Bank of Japan") (日本銀行), for example, is given as _NIPPON GINKO_ on banknotes but is often referred to, such as in the media, as _Nihon Ginkō_.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-nussbaum708-28)

**Nippon** is the form that is used usually or exclusively in the following constructions:[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-29)

- _Nippon Yūbin, Nippon Yūsei_ ( [Japan Post Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Post_Group "Japan Post Group"))
- _Ganbare Nippon!_ (A sporting cheer used at international sporting events, roughly, 'do your best, Japan!')
- _[Nipponbashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nipponbashi "Nipponbashi")_ (日本橋) (a shopping district in [Osaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka "Osaka"))
- [All Nippon Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Nippon_Airways "All Nippon Airways") ( _Zen Nippon Kūyu_)
- [Nikon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikon_Corporation "Nikon Corporation") ( _[Nippon Kōgaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_K%C5%8Dgaku "Nippon Kōgaku") Kōgyō_)
- [Nippon Animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Animation "Nippon Animation")
- [Nippon Ham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Ham "Nippon Ham")
- [Nippon Life Insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Life "Nippon Life")
- [Nippon Professional Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Professional_Baseball "Nippon Professional Baseball") ( _Nippon Yakyū Kikō_)
- [Nippon Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Steel "Nippon Steel") ( _Nippon Seitetsu_)
- [Nippon Telegraph and Telephone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Telegraph_and_Telephone "Nippon Telegraph and Telephone") ( _Nippon Denshin Denwa_)
- [Nippon Yusen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Yusen "Nippon Yusen")

**Nihon** is used always or most often in the following constructions:[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-30)

- _JR Higashi-Nihon_ ( [East Japan Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Japan_Railway "East Japan Railway"), [JR Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JR_Group "JR Group"))
- _[Nihonbashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihombashi "Nihombashi")_ (日本橋) (a bridge in [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo "Tokyo"))
- _Nihon Daigaku_ ( [Nihon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihon_University "Nihon University"))[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-31)
- _Nihon-go_ ( [Japanese language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language"))
- _Nihon-jin_ (日本人) ( [Japanese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_people "Japanese people"))[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-nussbaum708-28)
- _Nihon-kai_ ( [Sea of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan "Sea of Japan"))[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-32)
- _Nihon Kōkū_ ( [Japan Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Airlines "Japan Airlines"))
- _[Nihon-shoki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihon-shoki "Nihon-shoki")_ (an old history book, never _Nippon shoki_)[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-33)

- The [Nippon TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_TV "Nippon TV") network is called _Nihon Terebi_ in Japanese.

In 2016, element 113 on the [periodic table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_table "Periodic table") was named [nihonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihonium "Nihonium") to honor its discovery in 2004 by Japanese scientists at [RIKEN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIKEN "RIKEN").[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-34)

## Jipangu

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Names_of_Japan&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Jipangu")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0a/Map_of_America_by_Sebastian_Munster.JPG/250px-Map_of_America_by_Sebastian_Munster.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Map_of_America_by_Sebastian_Munster.JPG) Another spelling, "Zipangri" (upper left), was used on a 1561 map by [Sebastian Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_M%C3%BCnster "Sebastian Münster").[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-Forbes_JD_2007_21-35)

As mentioned above, the [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language "English language") word _Japan_ has a circuitous derivation; but [linguists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics "Linguistics") believe it derives in part from the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language "Portuguese language") recording of the [Early Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Mandarin "Early Mandarin") Chinese or [Wu Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Chinese "Wu Chinese") word for Japan: _Cipan_ (日本), which is rendered in [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin "Pinyin") as _Rìběn_ ( [IPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Phonetic_Alphabet "International Phonetic Alphabet"): ʐʅ˥˩pən˨˩˦), and literally translates to "sun origin". _Guó_ ( [IPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Phonetic_Alphabet "International Phonetic Alphabet"): kuo˨˦) is Chinese for "realm" or "kingdom", so it could alternatively be rendered as _Cipan-guo_. The word was likely introduced to Portuguese through the Malay: _Jipan_.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

Cipangu was first mentioned in Europe in the accounts of [Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo "Marco Polo").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-marco-polo-cipangu-7) It appears for the first time on a European map with the [Fra Mauro map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fra_Mauro_map "Fra Mauro map") in 1457, although it appears much earlier on Chinese and Korean maps such as the _[Gangnido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangnido "Gangnido")_. Following the accounts of Marco Polo, Cipangu was thought to be fabulously rich in silver and gold, which by Medieval standards was largely correct, owing to the volcanism of the islands and the possibility to access precious ores without resorting to (unavailable) deep-mining technologies.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

The modern [Shanghainese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghainese_dialect "Shanghainese dialect") pronunciation of Japan is _Zeppen_[\[zəʔpən\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Wu_Chinese "Help:IPA/Wu Chinese"). In modern Japanese, _Cipangu_ is transliterated as チパング which in turn can be transliterated into English as _Chipangu_, _Jipangu_, _Zipangu_, _Jipang_, or _Zipang_. _Jipangu_ (ジパング ( _Zipangu_)) as an [obfuscated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obfuscation "Obfuscation") name for Japan has recently\[ _[when?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")_\] come into vogue for Japanese [films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film "Film"), [anime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anime "Anime"), and [video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game "Video game").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

## Other names

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Names_of_Japan&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Other names")\]

### Classical

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Names_of_Japan&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Classical")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/Japan_yashima.png/250px-Japan_yashima.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Japan_yashima.png) Eight major islands of Japan

These names show up in historical texts for prehistoric legendary dates and also in names of gods and [Japanese emperors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Japanese_monarchs "List of Japanese monarchs"):

- _Ōyashima_ (大八洲) meaning the Great Country of Eight (or Many) Islands,[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-36) _[Awaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awaji_Island "Awaji Island")_, _Iyo_ (later [Shikoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikoku "Shikoku")), _[Oki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oki_Islands "Oki Islands")_, _Tsukushi_ (later [Kyūshū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ky%C5%ABsh%C5%AB "Kyūshū")), _[Iki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iki_Island "Iki Island")_, _[Tsushima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsushima_Island "Tsushima Island")_, _[Sado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sado,_Niigata "Sado, Niigata")_, and _Yamato_ (later [Honshū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honsh%C5%AB "Honshū")); [Hokkaidō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkaid%C5%8D "Hokkaidō"), [Karafuto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakhalin "Sakhalin"), [Chishima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chishima "Chishima"), and [Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Prefecture "Okinawa Prefecture") were not part of Japan in ancient times, as [Aynu Mosir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkaido "Hokkaido") (the northern part of the archipelago) was inhabited by a non-Japanese group, the [Ainu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ainu_people "Ainu people"). The eight islands refers to the creation of the main eight islands of Japan by the gods [Izanami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izanami "Izanami") and [Izanagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izanagi "Izanagi") in [Japanese mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_mythology "Japanese mythology") as well as the fact that eight was a synonym for "many".
- _Yashima_ (八島 or 八洲), "Eight (or Many) Islands"
- _[Fusō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusang "Fusang")_ (扶桑), a mythical tree or a mysterious land located to the east of China. The term later became a poetic name of Japan.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-37)
- _Mizuho_ (瑞穂) refers to ears of grain, e.g. 瑞穗國 _Mizuho-no-kuni_ "Country of Lush Ears (of Rice)". From Old Japanese _midu_ \> Japanese _mizu_ ("water; lushness, freshness, juiciness") + Old Japanese _fo_ \> Japanese _ho_ ("ear (of grain, especially rice)").
- _Shikishima_ (敷島) is written with Chinese characters that suggest a meaning "islands that one has spread/laid out", but this name of Japan supposedly originates in the name of an area in [Shiki District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiki_District,_Nara "Shiki District, Nara") of [Yamato Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamato_Province "Yamato Province") in which some emperors of ancient Japan resided. The name of Shikishima ( _i.e._ Shiki District) came to be used in Japanese poetry as an epithet for the province of Yamato ( _i.e._ the ancient predecessor of Nara Prefecture), and was [metonymically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metonymy "Metonymy") extended to refer to the entire island of Yamato ( _i.e._ Honshū) and, eventually, to the entire territory of Japan. The word _shima_, though generally meaning only "island" in [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language"), also means "area, zone, territory" in many [languages of the Ryūkyū Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyuan_languages "Ryukyuan languages").
- _Akitsukuni_ (秋津國), _Akitsushima_ (秋津島), _Toyo-akitsushima_ (豐秋津島). According to the literal meanings of the Chinese characters used to transcribe these names of Japan, _toyo_ means "abundant", _aki_ means "autumn", _tsu_ means "harbor", _shima_ means "island", and _kuni_ means "country, land". In this context, _-tsu_ may be interpreted to be a fossilized genitive case suffix, as in _matsuge_ "eyelash" (< Japanese _me_ "eye" + _-tsu_ \+ Japanese _ke_ "hair") or _tokitsukaze_ "a timely wind, a favorable wind" (< Japanese _toki_ "time" + _-tsu_ \+ Japanese _kaze_ "wind"). However, _akitu_ or _akidu_ are also archaic or dialectal Japanese words for " [dragonfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragonfly#In_culture "Dragonfly")", so "Akitsushima" may be interpreted to mean 秋津島 (_Dragonfly Island_).[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-38) Another possible interpretation would take _akitsu-_ to be identical with the _akitsu-_ of _akitsukami_ or _akitsumikami_ ("god incarnate, a manifest deity", often used as an honorific epithet for the [Emperor of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_of_Japan "Emperor of Japan")), perhaps with the sense of "the present land, the island(s) where we are at present".
- _Toyoashihara no mizuho no kuni_ (豐葦原の瑞穗の國). "Country of Lush Ears of Bountiful Reed Plain(s)", _[Ashihara no Nakatsukuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashihara_no_Nakatsukuni "Ashihara no Nakatsukuni")_, "Central Land of Reed Plains", "Country Amidst Reed Plain(s)" (葦原中國).
- _Hinomoto_ (日の本). Simple [kun reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kun_reading "Kun reading") of 日本.
- _Oyamoto_ (大和 _o + yamato_ "Great Yamato"), a literal Japanese reading, and _Daiwa_ (大和 _dai + wa_ "Great Wa"), a Sino-Japanese reading, are alternate readings of 大和, which is usually read as _Yamato_. Oyamoto is mostly used in historical or poetic contexts, while Daiwa is widely used in modern corporate names.

The [katakana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katakana "Katakana") [transcription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcription_(linguistics) "Transcription (linguistics)") ジャパン ( _Japan_) of the English word _Japan_ is sometimes encountered in Japanese, for example in the names of organizations seeking to project an international image. Examples include ジャパンネット銀行 ( _Japan Netto Ginkō_) (Japan Net Bank), ジャパンカップ ( _Japan Kappu_) (Japan Cup), ワイヤレスジャパン ( _Waiyaresu Japan_) (Wireless Japan), etc.

### In other Eastern Asian languages

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Names_of_Japan&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: In other Eastern Asian languages")\]

_Dōngyáng_ (東洋) and _Dōngyíng_ (東瀛) – both literally, "Eastern Ocean" – are [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language") terms sometimes used to refer to Japan exotically when contrasting it with other countries or regions in eastern [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia "Eurasia"); however, these same terms may also be used to refer to all of [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia") when contrasting "the East" with "the West". The first term, _Dōngyáng_, has been considered to be a pejorative term when used to mean "Japan", while the second, _Dōngyíng_, has remained a positive poetic name. They can be contrasted with _[Nányáng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanyang_(geographical_region) "Nanyang (geographical region)")_ (Southern Ocean), which refers to [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia"), and _Xīyáng_ (Western Ocean), which refers to the [Western world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_world "Western world"). In [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language") and [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language "Korean language"), the Chinese word for "Eastern Ocean" (pronounced as _tōyō_ in Japanese and as _dongyang_ (동양) in Korean) is used only to refer to the [Far East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_East "Far East") (including both East Asia and Southeast Asia) in general, and it is not used in the more specific Chinese sense of "Japan".

In [Mandarin Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese "Mandarin Chinese"), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan") is called _Rìběn_ 日本. The [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese "Cantonese") pronunciation of "日本" is _Yahtbún_\[jɐt˨pun˧˥\], the [Shanghainese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghainese "Shanghainese") pronunciation is _Zeppen_\[zəʔpən\], the [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkien "Hokkien") pronunciation is _Ji̍tpún_ or _Li̍t-pún_, the standard [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_language "Hakka language") pronunciation is _Ngi̍t-pún_ and the [Teochew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teochew_language "Teochew language") pronunciation is _Ji̍k púng_ . This has influenced the [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_language "Malaysian language") name for Japan, _Jepun_, and the [Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language "Thai language") word _Yipun_ (ญี่ปุ่น). The terms _Jepang_ and _Jipang_ were previously used in both Malay and Indonesian, but are today confined primarily to the [Indonesian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language "Indonesian language"). The Japanese introduced _Nippon_ and _Dai Nippon_ into Indonesia during the Japanese Occupation (1942–1945) but the native _Jepang_ remains more common. In Korean, Japan is called _Ilbon_ ( [Hangul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangul "Hangul"): 일본, [Hanja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanja "Hanja"): 日本), which is the Korean pronunciation of the [Sino-Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Korean_vocabulary "Sino-Korean vocabulary") name, and in [Sino-Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Vietnamese_vocabulary "Sino-Vietnamese vocabulary"), Japan is called _Nhật Bản_ (also rendered as _Nhựt Bổn_). In [Mongolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_language "Mongolian language"), Japan is called _Yapon_ (Япон).

_Ue-kok_ (倭國) is recorded for older [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkien "Hokkien") speakers.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Japan#cite_note-39) In the past, Korea also used 倭國, pronounced _Waeguk_ (왜국).
