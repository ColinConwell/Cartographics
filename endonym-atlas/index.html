Names, Languages & Sources

The same place can have many names. This atlas explores names used by the people and languages within a country.

What the Map Includes

Each country has an editorial default label. It does not mean that one language is more legitimate, universally spoken, or the only official language. Use the language selector to explore the included alternatives. Some governments use a colonial language; those locally used names are endonyms too.

The scope is countries, rather than every dependency or territory. Territories appear as geographic context without independent entries. Kosovo and Taiwan are explicitly identified; Palestine and Vatican City are included. Boundaries use generalized Natural Earth data and do not resolve disputed sovereignty, occupation, or control.

Three Ways to Read a Name

Native Script preserves local orthography. Romanized gives the name in Latin letters, retaining diacritics when available. For Latin-script names, the two are usually identical. Romanization systems differ by language; this is not a single uniform transliteration standard or an English translation.

Pronunciation is an editorial approximation for English readers, not a phonetic transcription. “ah” resembles the vowel in father, “ee” in see, “oo” in food, “uh” an unstressed vowel, “kh” a rough sound like Scottish loch, “gh” its voiced counterpart, and “q” a deeper back-of-mouth k. Capitals suggest stress where useful; they do not encode pitch accent or tone. English cannot reproduce every native sound, vowel length, or accent. These guides have not been reviewed by native speakers for every language.

When available, the detail panel shows source IPA and a real recording from the matching Wiktionary language section. IPA may list several dialects; consult its source for context. Recordings are not synthesized. Speaker nativeness and regional representativeness are not independently certified. Unavailable recordings are marked explicitly.

Research and Attribution

The primary reference is Wikipedia’s country endonym table, supplemented by country articles and the Names of Japan, Names of India, Names of China and Names of Korea articles. The list itself has editorial warnings and is a research starting point, not a linguistic authority.

Pronunciation lookups use Wiktionary. Recordings are bundled from Wikimedia Commons, with the original file, author and license linked next to each recording. Source material was retrieved on September 25, 2026. Per-country extracts, lookup results, editable data and build scripts are included in this folder.

Wikipedia and Wiktionary-derived text and this adapted name dataset are available under CC BY-SA 4.0; original contributors are credited through article history links. Audio keeps its individual license. Original interface code is MIT licensed. Natural Earth geometry is public domain; D3 and TopoJSON are locally bundled under their respective licenses. Country identifiers and coordinates are from mledoze/countries (ODbL).

Use and Reuse

Open index.html directly or serve the folder with any static web server. The map, names, code and recordings work offline; outbound source links require internet access. No analytics, API keys, accounts or external fonts are used. Local Noto font subsets support Tibetan, Thaana and Tifinagh; other scripts use your system fonts.
